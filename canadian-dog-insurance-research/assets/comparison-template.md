### Column legend (footnotes)

The numbering parallels the structure used by the source comparison doc; column meanings are adapted to Canadian regulatory and policy realities. See [`references/data-fields.md`](../references/data-fields.md) for the exact field type, normalization rule, and source-priority order behind each column.

1. **Underwriter** — Legal Canadian-licensed insurer carrying the risk (e.g., Northbridge, Trisura, Old Republic, Omega General). Distinct from the consumer-facing brand. `unknown` if not disclosed in policy wording.
2. **Provinces available** — ISO subdivision codes where the policy is sold (e.g., `ON, BC, AB, SK, MB, NB, NS, PE, NL`). Confirm via provider's province selector or licensing disclosure, not marketing copy.
3. **Provincial gaps** — Subdivisions explicitly excluded. Quebec is the most common gap (separate AMF licensing); territories often excluded.
4. **Billing currency** — `CAD` or `USD` (Trupanion historically billed CAD customers in USD on some plans — verify current state).
5. **Wording language** — `en` / `fr` / `both`. Quebec residents are entitled to French wordings under the Charter of the French Language; English copies may be courtesy translations only.
6. **Lifetime renewability** — `guaranteed` / `subject to underwriting` / `unknown`. Most Canadian insurers renew without re-underwriting but premiums climb with age.
7. **Max enrollment age** — Maximum age (years) at which a *new* policy can be opened. Often 8–14 depending on breed.
8. **Accident** — Coverage of traumatic, unexpected injury (e.g., foreign-body ingestion, fractures, lacerations).
9. **Illness** — Coverage of non-accident illness (infections, cancer, chronic disease, etc.).
10. **Hereditary & congenital** — Whether breed-related hereditary or congenital conditions (hip dysplasia, luxating patella, etc.) are covered, and whether enrollment must precede any clinical signs.
11. **Dental — illness** — Periodontal disease, extractions, tooth resorption. Many policies exclude unless an annual cleaning has been performed; capture the conditional phrase verbatim. Dental from accident is usually included by default and not broken out as a separate column.
12. **Behavioural** — Behavioural therapy / consultations (e.g., separation anxiety, reactivity).
13. **Alternative / holistic** — Acupuncture, chiropractic, hydrotherapy, physiotherapy, herbal/homeopathic. Each policy covers a slightly different subset.
14. **Exam fees** — Whether the consultation fee itself (not just diagnostics/treatment) is reimbursable on covered visits. Often an add-on.
15. **Prescription meds** — Vet-prescribed medications. Usually does **not** include all medications — covers commonly prescribed drugs only.
16. **Wellness add-on** — Optional preventive-care rider (vaccines, annual exam, dental cleaning, flea/tick). Closer to a payment plan than insurance; usually low cap.
17. **Direct vet pay** — Whether the insurer pays the clinic directly at point of care, so the owner doesn't front the full bill. Rare in Canada; Trupanion is the standout.
18. **Bilateral clause** — How conditions on the contralateral limb/eye/hip are treated. Many policies treat a second cruciate as related to the first if the first occurred during a defined window. Capture verbatim.
19. **Pre-existing handling** — Definition + curable-clause treatment (e.g., `excluded permanently`, `eligible after 18 months symptom-free except cruciate`, `curable yes, otherwise no`). Pull from policy wording, not marketing.
20. **Wait — accident (days)** — Days from policy effective date before accident claims become eligible. Commonly 2 days or 48 hours.
21. **Wait — illness (days)** — Days for illness claims. Commonly 14 days.
22. **Wait — orthopedic (days)** — Cruciate, hip, elbow, IVDD. Often 6 months (180 days). Some insurers waive with vet exam.
23. **Deductible model** — `annual` (most common), `per-condition` (Trupanion), `per-incident` (rare), `none`. The model materially changes lifetime cost for chronic conditions.
24. **Deductible options (CAD)** — Available deductible amounts as a list.
25. **Reimbursement options (%)** — Available reimbursement percentages (the share the insurer pays after deductible).
26. **Annual limit (CAD)** — Available annual payout caps; numeric, list of tiers, or `unlimited`. Note whether it is per-policy-year or calendar-year.
27. **Min monthly CAD — desk** — Lowest monthly premium published by the provider for the user's profile (high deductible, low reimbursement, low limit). `quote required — Phase 3` if the provider only exposes a quote tool. Not a personalized quote.
28. **Max monthly CAD — desk** — Highest monthly premium published for the same profile (low deductible, high reimbursement, high limit). Same caveats as `27`.
29. **Live monthly CAD** — **Phase 3 only.** Real CAD figure the user obtained from the provider's own quote tool, captured in [`04-quotes/<provider-slug>.md`](.). Never estimated. Until Phase 3 runs, value is `quote required — Phase 3`.
30. **Quote ref / expiry** — Quote reference number and expiry date as shown on the user's quote screen. `not shown` if the screen does not display them. Phase 3 only.
31. **Discounts** — Applicable discounts (multi-pet, bundle with home/auto, loyalty, alumni, employer, professional). Capture as comma-separated list with %.
32. **Notable exclusions** — Material exclusions worth surfacing (e.g., breeding/pregnancy, cosmetic procedures, prescription food, experimental treatment, behavioural specific to dangerous breeds). Verbatim where significant.
33. **Source URL + retrieved (UTC)** — Primary source URL the row's figures were drawn from, plus retrieval UTC timestamp. Multiple sources allowed — list newline-separated.

### Canadian adaptation notes (vs. the US source doc)

The source comparison doc contains 25 columns oriented around US insurers. The following adaptations apply in this skill's Canadian variant:

- **Renamed for currency / locale.** Source columns 23/24 (Min/Max cost per month USD) become `27`/`28` Min/Max monthly CAD.
- **Added Canadian-only columns.** `1` Underwriter (US doc treats brand and underwriter interchangeably), `2` Provinces available, `3` Provincial gaps, `4` Billing currency (USD/CAD distinction), `5` Wording language (Quebec French requirement), `6` Lifetime renewability, `17` Direct vet pay, `18` Bilateral clause, `30` Quote ref / expiry (for Phase 3 traceability), `33` Source URL + retrieved (UTC) (every figure must cite).
- **Dropped or merged.** Source columns for `Specialists`, `Hospitalization`, `Foreign Body Removal`, `Organ Transplants`, `Experimental`, `Preventable`, `Chronic`, `Physical Therapy`, `Televet` are subsumed under the Canadian columns `8` Accident, `9` Illness, `13` Alternative/holistic, or noted in `32` Notable exclusions. They are typically not the deciding factors in Canadian plan selection and bloat the table beyond legibility.
- **Phase 3 explicit.** The source doc reports a single price per insurer; this template makes the desk-vs-live distinction explicit (columns `27`/`28` vs. `29`/`30`) so the agent never silently mixes desk-research ranges with user-captured live quotes.
- **Source citation built in.** Every row carries a primary URL with a retrieval UTC stamp (column `33`); the source doc footnotes its single September 2024 retrieval globally. Canadian regulators move slowly but provider rate filings change frequently, so per-row timestamping is required.
